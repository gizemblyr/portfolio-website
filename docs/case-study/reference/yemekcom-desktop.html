Redesigning Yemek.com: A Modern Recipe Platform

The recipe app experience was reimagined to enhance clarity, improve navigation and foster user engagement through data-informed design.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Duration
6 months
Platform
Recipe platform, mobile app
Scope
Interviews, affinity mapping, IA, wireframes, design system, UI

At a glance

Problem

Users struggled to find relevant recipes, manage their collections and navigate the platform. Mobile sessions were 30% shorter than desktop.

What I did

Rebuilt search and filters, added a cooking mode, recipe lists, recipe creation and user profiles, on a scalable UI library.

Outcome

In usability testing, use of advanced search filters rose 32% and successful completion of key actions rose 28%.

Problem Goals Research Design Impact Next steps

01 Problem

Hard to Find, Hard to Keep, Hard to Use on a Phone

Yemek.com users faced significant challenges in discovering relevant recipes, managing their personal recipe collections and navigating the platform efficiently. The existing interface lacked modern design principles and failed to meet expectations for a contemporary recipe platform.

Current challenges

  • Complex and outdated user interface
  • Limited search and filtering
  • Poor recipe discovery and recommendations
  • Inadequate mobile experience and responsiveness
  • Lack of personalisation

Key pain points

Pain point Severity Evidence
Navigation difficulty High Interviews showed frequent confusion in navigating core categories
Search effectiveness High Many users needed multiple keywords to find relevant recipes
Mobile usability High Mobile sessions were 30% shorter than desktop
Recipe organisation Moderate No folders or tags for saved recipes
Recipe discovery Moderate Category pages had a bounce rate over 50%

Pain points

  • Recipe platforms are cluttered and hard to navigate

  • No filters for ingredients on hand or prep time
  • Long blog-style introductions before recipes
  • Mobile isn't optimised for use while cooking

App usage behaviour

  • Uses Yemek.com, Nefis Yemek Tarifleri and Instagram food creators
  • Searches by ingredient first ("What can I cook with eggplant and yogurt?")
  • Filters for "under 30 minutes" or "low calorie"
  • Scrolls for visuals before reading details

User interview insights

Interviews with 14 users based on the defined personas, to understand their cooking habits, app usage and expectations from a recipe platform.

  • Many users search by available ingredients, not just dish names
  • Categories like "quick meals" or "budget-friendly" were preferred over traditional ones
  • Users wanted to save recipes more easily and organise them into folders (e.g. "weeknight dinners", "diet meals")
  • Search and filters felt overwhelming or not specific enough
  • Strong preference for visual, step-by-step guidance while cooking
  • Few interact with the community, but they trust recipes with high ratings and real user photos

Affinity mapping

14 user interviews and over 100 user comments from Google Play, the App Store and other public platforms were grouped in an affinity map to uncover recurring patterns and prioritise design decisions.

Key findings

Discovery and search frustration

Limited filters and no visual categorisation made it hard to find things.

"I wish I could just filter by cooking time or ingredients I already have."

Organisation and recipe management

Users wanted better tools to save, organise and access favourite recipes.

"I save so many recipes, but can't find them later unless I scroll forever."

Cooking experience issues

Long recipe blocks were hard to follow while cooking; users preferred step-by-step instructions with images.

"I want clear steps and photos. I can't scroll and stir at the same time."

Desire for personalisation

High interest in features that adapt to dietary preferences or cooking goals.

"I'm vegan. I wish it didn't always show me meat recipes first."

Trust through community content

Recipes with user comments or photos were trusted more.

"If I see others tried it and shared pictures, I trust it more."

These insights formed the foundation of the design strategy and helped prioritise solutions for a more intuitive, personalised and motivating cooking experience.

04 Design

Design Solutions

Information architecture

Wireframes

Low-fidelity wireframes outlined core layouts and flows, focused on recipe discovery, search usability and personal recipe management. They validated ideas early and became the foundation for the final UI.

Design system

A scalable UI library to keep the app consistent.

Core components

  • Base styles for colour, typography and spacing
  • Core components like buttons, inputs and tags
  • App-specific elements such as recipe cards and timers

Implementation benefits

Responsive variants and interactive states supported mobile use and developer handoff. The system streamlined the UI process and supports future growth.

UI design

Home and search experience

Why

Limited filtering, non-visual category lists and a basic search made discovery hard. Comments and analytics showed users often abandoned search when they couldn't find something quickly or visually.

What improved

The filter panel allows multi-dimensional sorting (meal type, prep time, cooking method, diet). Categories were restructured visually for easier scanning, and smart search suggestions improve speed and accuracy.

Recipe details and cooking mode

Why

Users needed a clearer, distraction-free cooking flow and struggled with long recipe texts. The old layout didn't track steps, didn't surface similar recipes and hid community feedback.

What improved

  • A scrollable cooking mode with step-by-step instructions
  • Clearer layout for ingredients and timing
  • User comments and similar recipes highlighted
  • Clear "Start" and "Finish" calls to action

Recipe management and lists

Why

Users wanted a better way to organise and revisit recipes. The old system had no grouping, no public sharing and no easy access to saved content.

What improved

  • Custom recipe lists with editable titles
  • A favourites tab for quick access
  • Public/private toggle for shared inspiration
  • A clean, visual layout for list previews

Recipe creation and sharing

Why

  • Users needed a structured, flexible way to share recipes
  • The existing flow lacked step-by-step clarity and visual cues
  • There was no space for tips or serving suggestions
  • Users wanted reassurance their content wouldn't be lost mid-entry

What improved

  • Multiple steps with optional images
  • Optional fields for tips, serving suggestions and an intro
  • Step-by-step navigation with Back and Next
  • Auto-save with a visible timestamp

User profiles and social features

Why

  • Users wanted to showcase shared recipes under their own profiles
  • There was no way to explore others' collections or follow authors
  • Lack of social presence reduced engagement and repeat visits
  • Users asked for a simple space to edit their profile and track content

What improved

  • Public profiles with follower and following counts
  • Recipe lists others can browse and save
  • A clear toggle between "Shared Recipes" and "Recipe List"
  • Social links plus "Edit Profile" and "Follow" buttons

Key learnings

Personalisation matters

Ingredient-based search and "just for you" sections resonated strongly.

Structured cooking mode

A visually clear cooking mode improves usability and reduces cognitive load, especially on mobile.

Social features drive engagement

Sharing and following build long-term engagement when they are easy to discover.

Small UI improvements matter

A favourites tab or recipe folders can significantly affect retention when tied to real user needs.
