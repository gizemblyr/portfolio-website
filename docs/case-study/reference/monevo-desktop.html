Monevo: Gamified Financial Habits

Designing a gamified approach to financial wellness that helps users distinguish between wants and needs while building healthy spending habits through engaging interactions.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Duration
3 months
Platform
Mobile app
Scope
Benchmarking, journey mapping, wireframes, usability testing

At a glance

Problem

Over half of new users left at the welcome screen, and only 8.1% completed tasks. Onboarding asked for everything upfront.

What I did

Rebuilt onboarding as a short gamified task series, added a reward store and goal-based task flows, and tested two homepage structures.

Outcome

In usability testing with 7 participants, onboarding completion reached 70% and task completion 15%.

04 Research

Research and User Insights

Benchmarking

Key insights

  • Duolingo's gamification model proves highly effective for habit formation
  • Cleo's conversational interface engages users but lacks comprehensive tools
  • Revolut offers robust features but can overwhelm new users

Our differentiators

  • Focus on wants vs. needs categorisation
  • Educational content integrated into daily interactions
  • Potential for cashback and brand-based rewards, offering real-world value unlike most competitor apps

User personas

Home economists

32–50 years old

Users managing family finances, looking for practical savings tips and stress-free budgeting tools.

Professionals

23–32 years old

Career-driven individuals paying off debt or loans and seeking smart financial management. They tend to buy more than they need and are motivated by rewards and goal tracking.

These personas, based on client-led research, ensured the onboarding, gamified task system and financial content were tailored to diverse user needs.

User journey mapping

Users faced unclear guidance, overwhelming onboarding, low motivation, confusing visual design and a lack of understanding around key features like the Monevo Score. The research highlighted the need for simpler language, streamlined flows, personalised goal setting and a clearer visual hierarchy.

05 Strategy

Design Strategy

Progressive learning

Broke complex financial concepts into small, manageable steps to reduce cognitive load.

Gamification

Built habit loops using points, badges and rewards to drive ongoing engagement.

Personalisation

Tailored tasks and goals to user types, aligned with AI logic through collaboration with developers.

Visual clarity

A clean visual hierarchy and chat-based guidance improved comprehension and ease of use.

Guided structure

After testing sequential and open task flows, we chose a structured approach that supports goal setting and reduces decision fatigue.

Key design solutions

  • Step-by-step onboarding clarified concepts like wants vs. needs, reducing drop-off
  • A gamified task system with points, rewards and progress flow increased engagement and completion
  • Clear visual hierarchy and chat cues improved navigation and understanding
  • Personalised task flows based on user types, aligned with AI through dev collaboration
  • Motivational chat reminders helped users stay on track with spending and tasks
  • Enhanced reports showed key spending categories, completed tasks and earned points
  • Reward choice at the end of a series boosted autonomy and motivation

06 Design & Testing

Validated Through Usability Testing

Welcome screens

Why

The old welcome screen lacked clarity and failed to explain the app's value, leading to early drop-offs. We redesigned it to showcase key features like gamification and financial education using visuals and app previews, so users quickly understand what to expect.

Testing results

  • Before: over 50% did not proceed after the first open (live)
  • After: 70% of participants continued into the first series
  • 85.7% described it as "inviting" or "clearer than expected"

What users said

  • "I understood what the app was about before signing up, that made a difference."
  • "The visuals helped me see that it wasn't just another finance app."
  • "I felt like I was being introduced, not just dropped into something."

Gamified onboarding via "Getting to know you" series

Why

The old app asked for personal and financial information right after opening, leading to a 52.6% drop-off at the welcome screen. We restructured onboarding as a gamified task series: small interactive tasks (e.g. "Tell us about your financial goals") that earn points. This reduced friction and gave users an early sense of achievement.

Testing results

  • Onboarding completion: 47.4% → 70% (+47.6%)
  • Welcome screen drop-off: 52.6% → under 30%
  • 85.7% called onboarding "clear and engaging"

What users said

  • "It felt like a journey, not a boring form."
  • "I wanted to keep going to see what came next and get the points."
  • "Much better than being asked for everything upfront. I actually completed it."

My Monevo World

Why

Created to help users visually track progress and stay motivated through a gamified journey. It also serves users who prefer a practical, finance-focused approach: quick actions like viewing insights, accessing expert advice or buying precious metals, without relying on gamification.

Testing results

  • 85.7% accessed and explored My Monevo World
  • 85.7% understood their progress and rewards
  • Task motivation increased, based on post-task interviews

What users said

  • "I loved seeing my progress. It felt like I was actually getting somewhere."
  • "This made the app feel more fun and less stressful."
  • "I like that I can both track and take action without digging through menus."

Receipt-based expense input

Why

Manual expense entry was time-consuming and often skipped. A receipt-based option lets users upload or scan receipts directly, which also helps with shared or recurring expenses.

Testing results

  • 71.4% preferred receipt input over manual entry
  • 85.7% found it "easy" or "very easy"
  • 72% said it made monthly tracking feel more reliable

What users said

  • "Uploading a receipt felt faster and easier than typing everything."
  • "It's helpful when I split bills with someone."
  • "This saves me from forgetting what I spent on."

Goal-based task series and reward system

Why

Users earned points but couldn't spend them, so the system felt empty. We added a store to redeem points and goal-based series (e.g. expense tracking for users paying off debt), each with 5–6 short tasks and mini quizzes. At the end, users get a congratulation, points and a reward of their choice.

Testing results

  • Task completion within series: 8.1% → 15% (+85.2%)
  • 71.4% preferred series over isolated tasks

What users said

  • "It was satisfying to finish a full series. It felt like a win."
  • "I actually care about the points now because I can use them."
  • "Choosing my own reward made it feel more personal and fun."

Testing two homepage variants

Why

The original homepage lacked structure; users didn't know where to start. We tested two variants: sequential task cards and open access task blocks, to find the layout that best supports engagement and navigation.

Testing results

  • 3 of 7 preferred sequential tasks
  • 2 of 7 preferred open access
  • 1 liked both equally
  • Decision: sequential layout, to guide users step by step and reduce decision fatigue

What users said

  • "The step by step layout made me feel more focused."
  • "I liked having everything visible, but I wasn't sure where to begin."
  • "The card based version felt more like a journey, which helped me stay on track."

Feature enhancements based on testing

Most-spent category in reports

Users wanted a clearer view of where they overspent. Reports now highlight the most-spent category with clear labels and visual cues.

"I want to know instantly where my money went the most."

Expected impact

Stronger spending awareness and better monthly planning, by drawing attention to problem areas without overwhelming users.

Smart reminders via chat assistant

Push notifications were mostly ignored. In-app chat reminders, triggered by unclassified spending or inactivity, felt more personal.

"Push notifications feel robotic, but in-chat felt like someone was guiding me."

Expected impact

Higher task completion, more accurate expense categorisation and less drop-off in journey-based task flows.

07 Impact

Impact and Key Learnings

Improved task and onboarding completion

Usability tests showed a clear increase in task completion during onboarding. Step-by-step guidance and integrated task flows helped users complete more actions independently and with greater confidence. In testing, 85.7% of participants completed onboarding and explored key features.

Purposeful gamification

Gamified elements boosted engagement only when tied to clear goals, real rewards and visible progress. Decorative gamification alone was not effective.

Clarity leads to action

Users responded better to visual insights than raw data. Features like "Most spent category" and a clear hierarchy helped users take action and stay engaged.
